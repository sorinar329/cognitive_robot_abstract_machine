The turbine outside

Tower tapering from 5.99 m to 3.0 m, three 63 m blades lofted from the IEA chord, twist and airfoil data (tilted 5°, coned 3°), nacelle with weather mast and aviation light.

Tower base foundation · entrance

Concrete plinth flush with the ground, grout joint, base flange with 96 anchor nuts, earthing strap and the entrance door at ground level, so the G1 can walk straight in. This is where it does its first round.

Inside the tower platforms · lift · yaw deck

Rest platforms 1.1 m under each flange joint, a ladder with fall-arrest rail, power cables, wall lamps and a service lift that runs 105 m from the ground floor to the yaw deck, 2.6 m under the tower top. From there the power cables hang in a loop through the deck, so the nacelle can yaw.

Nacelle IEA layout · cover removed

Along the 5° tilted shaft: two main bearings, three-stage gearbox (1:97), fast shaft with brake and coupling, DFIG generator; behind it the transformer and the converter and controller cabinets. Walkways are 0.69 m wide.

Unitree G1 recorded in CRAMERA

The G1 is planned and moved by CRAM (coraplex planner, giskardpy controller) and recorded with CRAMERA’s onboarder. At each inspection point it walks to a standpoint and aims its head camera, a D435 tilted 48° down.

  • CameraSees only things at or below its own height (1.27 m): the waist bends at most 0.52 rad.
  • TasksT1 and T2 inspect; T3 assists a technician (pick up, carry, set down); T4 and T5 get it up the turbine; T6 checks the tower flange bolts.
  • FindingsTaken from the scenario’s ground truth for now; recognising faults in camera images is the next step.

Getting up lift and crane hoist

The G1 walks on flat floors only. The ladder is out of reach for it, and the opening from the yaw deck into the nacelle is taken up by the drivetrain. So it rides: the tower lift gets it to the yaw deck, and the nacelle’s own service crane gets it into the nacelle. The crane lowers a lifting platform through a hatch in the rear floor. In CRAM the robot’s odom frame is attached to the moving platform or lift car, and one joint motion drives the hoist.

Bolt check T6 · flange bolts up the tower

The G1 rides the lift and stops at the three rest platforms. On each one it walks a ring of standpoints and reads the torque markings on the nuts under the flange: 132 M42 bolts on flanges 1 and 2, 100 M36 on flange 3. The IEA reference gives no bolt data, so these are typical sizes. It then rides down, walks out and reports.

__BOLT_REPORT__
  • TightnessA nut that turns back loses preload. Turning it back by the angle that tightened it from snug to full preload (105° for M42, 117° for M36, from bolt and flange stiffness) releases all of it. So preload ≈ 1 − marking offset / that angle. The tightness ratio is the mean over the bolts read.
  • LimitsACP RP 401 thresholds, borrowed from anchor bolts: any bolt below 85 % or a mean below 90 % → re-tension the whole flange. That is why flange 2 is re-tensioned although its mean is near 99 %.
  • Not seenThe G1’s camera sits 0.34 m above the nuts and must look past the flange edge. Bolts behind the lift shaft go on the technician’s list. On flange 2, one turned nut (bolt 60) is back there.
  • Limits of markingsThey only show nuts that turned. Preload lost by settling doesn’t move them, so a 10 % tension sample stays in the plan. Readings come from the scenario ground truth for now; visibility is computed in the CRAM world.

Physics check MuJoCo replay of the CRAM runs

Each recorded CRAM run is played again in MuJoCo, the way CRAMERA’s lab does it. The G1 is a rigid-body model with the URDF’s masses and collision meshes. Every joint is a servo limited to its real torque: 25 Nm for the arms, 1.4 Nm for the fingers. The pelvis follows the recorded path (no balance controller, so it cannot fall). The turbine is solid, and the tool case is a free 3 kg box held only by friction. The replay uses CRAM’s own motion, without the walking animation.

__PHYSICS__
  • FixedThe replay found flaws the CRAM plans could not see, because straight base moves don’t avoid collisions. T3 and T4 clipped the tool-rack corner. T4 stepped over a 30 cm bedplate girder, so the rear frame now ends behind the slip ring. T5 left the lift car through its side railing. T2’s standpoints put the hips on the bedplate girder.
  • OpenThe G1’s grasp for the tool case (a CRAM grasp setting for the Dex3 hand). The walkway is 0.69 m wide while the parked hands span about 0.66 m, so the hands brush the wall, and the head touches it when the waist leans back.
  • ModelReal walking (a balance policy) is not included: the pelvis is held on the recorded path. Masses of turbine parts don’t matter here; they are fixed.
